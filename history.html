LOCAL_HISTORY: VALENCIA_6215

PRE-1856 // THE ERA OF ERMITA

The Mountain Sanctuary

Originally, the town was known as Ermita ("Hermitage"). During the 19th century, coastal residents of Negros Oriental were under constant threat from Moro pirate raids. The lush, mist-covered mountains of Valencia provided the perfect high-altitude refuge where families could hide and defend themselves far from the shoreline.

AUGUST 28, 1856 // SPANISH ESTABLISHMENT

Nueva Valencia

The town was formally organized by Spanish authorities in 1856. It was renamed Nueva Valencia by the first parish priest, Father Matias Villamayor. He named it after his hometown in Spain. The iconic sunken fountain in the town plaza—which you can still see today—was a gift imported directly from Spain during this era.

1905 - 1948 // THE NAME CHANGE

The Luzuriaga Interim

In 1905, the town’s name was changed to Luzuriaga in honor of Don Carlos Ruiz de Luzuriaga, a prominent Negros legislator. While the name was official for over 40 years, the local people always felt a deep connection to their original identity. After years of petitioning, the name was finally restored to Valencia on October 11, 1948, via Republic Act No. 294.

1942 - 1945 // WWII RESISTANCE

Seat of the "Free Government"

Valencia was the most vital strategic point in the province during WWII. It served as the headquarters for the Free Government of Negros Oriental under Governor G.Z. Villanueva. While the Japanese occupied Dumaguete, the resistance operated from the safety of Valencia’s rugged hills.

This history is preserved at the Cata-al World War II Museum in town, housing one of the most significant private collections of war relics in the Philippines.

1983 - PRESENT // ENERGY & ENVIRONMENT

The Geothermal Heart

Today, Valencia is known as the "Little Baguio" of Negros due to its cool climate. It is home to the Palinpinon Geothermal Power Plant, which provides clean energy to the entire Visayas region. It has been repeatedly awarded as the "Cleanest and Greenest" town in the province, maintaining its legacy as a mountain paradise.
